Methodology

This project measures what share of posts in each year carried a given tag. Sample: 463164 posts, 2011–2026.

Where the year comes from

The rule34.xxx API exposes no upload date. The only timestamp in a post object is change, the time of the last edit, and it is always greater than or equal to the upload time. Posts are sorted by ID and IDs grow monotonically, so the year is reconstructed from the ID: ID windows are matched against the minimum change in the window, the series is smoothed monotonically (pool-adjacent-violators), and each post is then assigned to a year by interpolation.

This is an estimate. Retagging old posts drags it forward, and at year boundaries the error can reach several months.

Early years are thin: there were fewer uploads before 2014, and a random sample rarely lands there. The left edge of every chart is noisy, and those shares are a rough guide, not a measurement.

Margin of error

The “±” column is a 95% confidence interval computed as 1.96·√(p(1−p)/n) over the last three years. At a 10% share and n=3000 that is ±1.1 pp. A difference smaller than the interval is not a signal.

Artifacts

The “jump” column is the largest one-year rise in share. Values above 8 are dropped: that profile means a tag was introduced or retagged in bulk, not that tastes shifted. Example — genitals: 0.4% in 2020 and 22% in 2021, a 54-fold rise.

Gradual changes in tagging convention slip past the filter. The fall of human mostly means people stopped listing the species, not that fewer humans appear in the images.

What is not measured

Share is neither quality nor views. score is useless for this: votes are rare and values are 0 or 1. Deleted posts never enter the sample, so the history is slightly censored by moderation. The ai_generated share is applied by hand, so it understates the real figure.
